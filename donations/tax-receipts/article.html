Tax Receipts

Generate and manage tax receipts for donors.

By MAC Administration Last updated:

Tax receipts are important records that donors receive for their financial contributions. Since people choose to donate through various methods, the process for issuing these receipts differs depending on the specific channel used.

When Are Tax Receipts Sent Out?

  • Tax receipts are distributed annually every February, which aligns smoothly with the start of the annual tax filing season.
  • The distribution period typically runs from the first week of February all the way through the end of the month.

1. The Online Donation Portal

  • When donors use our online donation portal to contribute with their credit cards, they receive their tax receipts automatically.
  • This seamless process occurs since the online checkout form collects all necessary details upfront, including the donor's full name, email address, and complete mailing address.
  • Due to this automatic data collection, the system triggers an email instructing the donor to set up a donor profile.
  • This donor profile securely saves their address and personal information, which are required to successfully generate and send out their tax receipt.

2. E-Transfers and Regular Donations

  • Donors can also receive tax receipts for regular contributions made through e-transfers, credit cards, and other standard payment methods.
  • These contributions are linked to a donor profile that keeps track of the individual's giving history, contact information, and address details.
  • However, there is an important rule to keep in mind: if a donor uses an alternate payment method or a brand-new credit card for an e-transfer or online payment, they must properly connect that new method to their profile.
  • Completing this connection ensures that the system recognizes the updated payment path and matches it to the correct profile so that the tax receipt is issued accurately.

3. Card Machines (Clover and Stripe S-700 Terminals)

  • Donations made through physical point-of-sale card machines—such as Clover terminals or Stripe S-700 devices—are completely anonymous.
  • While the machine prints out a standard payment slip, it does not capture the donor's name, email, or personal details.
  • Consequently, if a donor wants a tax receipt for a machine payment, they must complete several manual steps:
  1. First, the donor needs to fill out a physical pledge form for their specific center, making sure to write down all their personal information and email details.
  2. Next, they must securely staple their printed payment receipt directly to that completed form.
  3. Finally, this physical package must be submitted to the accounting department so they can process the tax receipt manually.

4. QMac Kiosks (Standalone Touchscreen Machines)

  • QMac kiosks are standalone touchscreen machines with a green background that can be found in masjids and administrative offices.
  • To make a contribution, a person simply selects a campaign and taps their payment card on the screen.
  • If the donor types in their email address, the system automatically sends them an email message containing a link to set up a donor profile.
  • Setting up this profile requires the donor to provide their email, first name, last name, and complete home address.
  • Once that profile is finished, the payment card they used at the kiosk becomes permanently linked to their account, meaning they only have to complete this setup one single time for all future donations.

Important

We specifically need the donor's email address to handle kiosk contributions properly. Immediately after the payment finishes, the screen prompts the donor to type in their email address.

Unfortunately, many donors skip this step and walk away, which leaves their email and contact information uncollected. Without this information, they cannot receive an automated profile link or a tax receipt.
